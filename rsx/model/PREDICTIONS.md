## Habit (trigger → routine)

With η = 0.035 per trigger-paired run, automaticity reaches 0.5 after 20 runs, 0.8 after 46 and 0.95 after 85. At about one real trigger a day this is in the range Lally et al. (2010) observed for everyday habits (median 66 days to 95% of asymptote, range 18–254).

## Design consequences to test

1. **Two separate outcomes.** The reflex (trigger starts the routine) is a habit; the change in how the trigger feels is counterconditioning. Measure both: latency/SRBAI and trigger distress *before* running the routine.
2. **Changing the trigger needs more than pairing** (P3–P5). Candidate additions to test: interleave stages A and B rather than finishing A first; begin each run with a brief, tolerable moment of the trigger alone before "I move, I act" (in this model, non-reinforced trigger exposure is what moves its value toward zero); fade the bundle to fewer channels once the routine is reliable.
3. **Test cues alone and together.** A single-channel recall test after training is a direct check of P2.
4. **H3 is a two-sided bet.** The model predicts faster state induction with more channels (P1) but less trigger revaluation (P5). A human study that measures both outcomes can tell which matters more.

Model limits: Rescorla–Wagner treats each run as one compound trial, so it ignores that the trigger comes *before* the routine (serial order), as well as context, spontaneous recovery and reconsolidation. Real saliences are unknown and differ between people.
