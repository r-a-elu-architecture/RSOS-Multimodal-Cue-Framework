00 / Mission and research contract

What we are trying
to discover—and prove.

The overarching RSOS ambition is to uncover potentially exceptional mechanisms in the evolution of human–AI interactions. This lab evaluates one tractable part of that ambition: whether explicit structural and ancestry information improves auditable prediction and provenance beyond strong generic alternatives.

Research mission document ↗

Mission: reconstruct the hidden history of ideas, not hidden model internals.

Trace original authorship, operator inheritance, revisions, re-entry and recombination across historical dialogue. Explain which structures predict later observed behavior, distinguish reuse from coincidence or repetition, and test whether the reconstruction creates operational value in AI memory, audit and decision support. Any proposed world-first mechanism remains a hypothesis until adversarially validated.

Current state / precise scope

Evidence dashboard, not a training runner.

Displayed operator scores are previously reported retrospective findings on a selected archive. The local CSV tool computes Brier for visitor-provided predictions; it cannot independently reconstruct the research or establish a held-out split. Ancestry proof ratios compare two internal versions.

Source material: 9,000 selected messages; 25-conversation strict holdout with 259 response pairs; no untouched external corpus replication reported.

Research question

Does structural history add information?

Can provenance-aware operator representations predict subsequent recorded assistant-language events better than equally informed contextual text and retrieval systems, without duplicate leakage?

Scientific goals

Recover → explain → test → apply.

Reconstruct origin and descendants; test candidate mechanisms using ablations and counterfactuals; measure real predictive or verification improvements; build an auditable product only after independent validation.

What qualifies as success

Independent, matched, reproducible advantage.

Predeclare splits and metrics, audit near duplicates, match competitor input access, publish paired effects and cluster-aware uncertainty, and replicate across untouched datasets against strong modern baselines.

What would falsify a claim

When matched alternatives explain the gain.

Any advantage lost after stronger text/retrieval controls, leakage removal, external replication or proper proof-cost accounting is a constraint on that particular mechanism—not proof of architectural superiority.

Relationship to Cue Lab: this lab studies recorded structural ancestry and its predictive/engineering value. Cue Lab v1.3 independently tests synthetic compositional cue transfer; synthetic regret cannot establish historical-ancestry discovery or human conditioning. 04 / Evidence integrity

Ambition requires
an audit trail.

Every performance figure needs a defensible origin, relevant competition and explicit scope. The lab separates observed retrospective results from scientific hypotheses.

What is presently supported

  • Operator-aware representation improved lexical forecasting on the reported strict historical holdout.
  • A generic equal-context lexical model provides a meaningful internal control.
  • Ancestry proofs were smaller than the project's earlier internal implementation in three reported cohorts.
  • Additional pairwise operator complexity did not consistently improve accuracy.

What remains unproven

  • External generalization across untouched conversational corpora.
  • Superiority to modern, optimized transformer or retrieval baselines.
  • Modification of internal model weights or emergence of a novel neural mechanism.
  • Clinical efficacy, commercialization impact or world-first exclusivity.

Inspect the research. Challenge the architecture.

Trace the historical experiments to their written evidence, reproduce calculations where data permits, and preserve negative results. The cue-transfer simulation remains a separate research track, not the lead claim of this lab.
