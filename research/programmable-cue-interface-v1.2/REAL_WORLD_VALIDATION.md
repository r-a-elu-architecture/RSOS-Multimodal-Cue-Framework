# Path from programmable prototype to scientific evidence

## Phase A — nonclinical feasibility, no distress induction

Participants voluntarily register personally meaningful neutral images, sounds, gestures or private mnemonic glyphs and explicitly confirm meanings. Collect baseline cue-recognition latency, recall accuracy, contextual prompt relevance, opt-out rate, and subjective preference ratings. EEG can optionally be collected as an auxiliary signal; labels must come from measured task outcomes or user reports, not assumed alpha/beta emotional states. No smell or taste exposure until explicit independent consent and allergy/comfort screening.

Conditions should include (randomized within participants and counterbalanced): self-chosen calibrated cue program, equally expressive generic adaptive cue program, fixed cue program, and yoked timing with equal exposure. Hold training experiences separate from 24h/7d follow-up tests, switch context, include sham/neutral cues and opt-out measures. Stratify per person so personal glyphs cannot leak between users. The objective measures should be recall or performance on a neutral task; **do not induce clinical trauma**.

## Phase B — independent evaluation of voluntary resource-state cueing

Only with qualified independent ethics oversight, user choice and proper endpoint validation. Pre-register intervention operators, sequence order, primary behavioral endpoint, adverse-event/withdrawal rules, allowable decision budget, exact baselines, subgroup uncertainty, and the change protocol for stopping. A research pilot is not proof of PTSD treatment; EMDR should not be implemented by analogy to software icons.

## Current missing data

No individual longitudinal intervention-response dataset under the proposed seven modules; no recording in this environment of EEG during personalized cue exposure; no measured delayed response to conditioned glyph/sound chains; no independent laboratory replication. Public motor-imagery EEG data are suitable for signal-processing smoke tests, not for asserting cue-response efficacy.
