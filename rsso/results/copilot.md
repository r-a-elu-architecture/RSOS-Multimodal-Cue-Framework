## Typology from speech (claim C18)

Speech with a known lean: one word in ten is a type word, half of those from the true type. A profile needs ten type words, and the leading type must be ahead of the next by at least twice the square root of the type words (so chance alone rarely names one).

| Plug-in | Words of speech | Right type | Wrong type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sensory words | 200 | 61% | 0% |
| Sensory words | 500 | 98% | 0% |
| Sensory words | 1000 | 100% | 0% |
| Time orientation | 200 | 61% | 0% |
| Time orientation | 500 | 98% | 0% |
| Time orientation | 1000 | 100% | 0% |

With no lean (1,000 words), a type was named in 3% (sensory words) and 3% (time orientation).

## Cue-reactivity over months (claims C4, C31)

Craving rated at three cue exposures a week for three months, whole points with noise (sd 1.5).

| Truth | Called falling | Called rising |
|---|---|---|
| Falls 1 point a month | 96% | 0% |
| No change | 6% | 2% |

Reading: the copilot finds the cue lines it has words for and misses paraphrases, so its agreement with a therapist is limited by both; a matrix should grow its word list from real sessions. The level classifier works on clear sentences but is a word-list method, not an understanding of the sentence. A typology needs hundreds of words of speech before it names a type, and with no lean it rarely names one. A real fall in cue-reactivity of one point a month is found within three months.
