# Decisive, ethically bounded first human experiment

## Objective

Test **neutral memory/skill cueing**, not trauma therapy, belief implantation, covert persuasion, or clinical outcomes. Can user-calibrated visual/sound cue chains improve objective recall and reaction time relative to fixed cues and a matched generic adaptive controller?

## Participants and protocol

Recruit consenting adults with appropriate institutional review and safeguarding, and set sample size by a power analysis based on pre-specified smallest interesting effect. A 10–20-participant usability pilot can identify feasibility problems, **not** provide definitive efficacy evidence.

1. Explicit user-chosen goal: memorize arbitrary neutral symbol–word pairings (or neutral skill responses) across several contexts.
2. Randomly sample candidate glyphs and sounds; collect an individual preference/cue-recognition calibration and optional EEG/HRV with artifact and confidence flags. Never equate alpha/beta with emotions or self-reported beliefs.
3. Randomize *equal-duration* arms: fixed visual prompt; conventional adaptive cue; user-personalized single glyph; user-personalized two-glyph chain; two-glyph + neutral future-context rehearsal. Preserve actual matched intervention time and number of exposures.
4. Use **identity-blind, pre-registered** recall accuracy and response latency at immediate, +24h, +7d follow-ups. Alternate context deliberately. Require a stop option and never target distressing personal memories without appropriate clinical supervision.
5. Split by participant and task to avoid leakage. Run correct graph versus deliberately scrambled graph negative control only when harmless (neutral mnemonic contexts), and verify all individual cue mappings were authorized.
6. Strong generic adaptive control receives identical feedback, hardware, contexts, and calibration. The exact-match controller provides a necessary architectural equivalence check.

## Primary hypothesis

Personalized compositional cue programs will improve *objective delayed recall or latency* relative to the strongest generic adaptive controller at equal cue exposure. If they do not, the claim of incremental efficacy is falsified for this setting.

## Study prerequisites

IRB or equivalent ethics approval; protocol registration; participant privacy; hardware calibration and blinded analysis; measurement reliability; safe stopping; real human outcome data; plain-language disclosure that this is experimental. A controlled clinical treatment study would be a distinct subsequent program with clinical expertise and validated instruments.
