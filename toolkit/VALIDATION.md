# rsos_cue — validation

## 1. Synthetic run logs (current)

`validate/runlog_eval.py`, results in `validate/results/runlog_eval.csv`. Run logs are generated by `examples/simulate_runlog.py`: 60 invented people per seed, three hidden response types that differ in which protocol, tier and voice help them and how that depends on the trigger (sound, smell, sight, memory). For 30 held-out people per seed (3 seeds), the engine gets a budget of runs and recommends one setting per trigger type.

Regret = true best gain for that trigger minus the true gain of the recommended setting, in rating points (lower is better). Mean ± standard error:

| Runs per new person | Population best (fixed) | Engine, random runs | Engine, its own probes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 1.10 ± 0.04 | 0.76 ± 0.04 | 0.84 ± 0.04 |
| 10 | 1.10 ± 0.04 | 0.57 ± 0.04 | **0.44 ± 0.03** |
| 20 | 1.10 ± 0.04 | 0.32 ± 0.03 | **0.24 ± 0.02** |

- After 10 runs the engine's choice loses 60% less than giving everyone the setting that is best on average; after 20 runs, 78% less.
- From 10 runs on, choosing what to try next beats trying settings at random (−22% and −26%). With only 5 runs, random runs are slightly better, because the engine spends its first 4 runs exploring.
- **Limit:** the invented response types are additive, which matches the engine's model. Real people will be less tidy; these numbers show the machinery works, not how large real benefits are.
