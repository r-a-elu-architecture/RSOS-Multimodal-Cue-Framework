Software agents and a simulated person, run by one architecture.

On the left, six software agents depend on each other and fail. On the right, a simulated person meets cues that raise distress. The same Watchdog chooses each side's working mode every tick, and the Spine and the safety layer can overrule it.

Evidence: E4 Tested on recorded data. In simulation, 27 registered tests: 1 pass, 7 mixed, 18 fail, 1 analysis; on recorded data, 15 registered tests: 0 pass, 4 mixed, 7 fail, 4 other. Not yet tested with people using RSOS.

These are replays of real runs of the tested code (rsxv: World, AgentEnvV2, PersonEnvV2), recorded tick by tick. Both sides are stated models, not people and not production systems. Choose an intervention and a run, press play, and click the timeline to see why any decision was made.

        What you are looking at

        The Watchdog estimates, from what it has seen so far, which mode fits each context, and switches through a two-phase handover. It also micro-randomises some ticks to keep learning. On the person side, a safety mode such as STABILISE blocks deep work whatever the Watchdog wants, and the run falls back to stabilising. Each block shows as a small gold mark on the timeline and as a step in the decision chain for that tick.

        Results of every registered test, passes and failures, are on the results page. The result most relevant here is T-AP: a policy learned on agents passed its registered test on the simulated person, but it fails the baseline audit, because always stabilising did better.

        Person Lab: 100 lab persons, and agents working toward each one's desired state

        Each lab person has an identity profile on six levels, from environment up to purpose, with where they are now and where they want to be. Choose one and run a session: every tick the Watchdog picks an archetype (Bear steadies, Raven clarifies, Tiger takes a step, Lion shares), and each action is an RSX-V program whose TARGET line names the part of the profile it serves. Always Bear, the simple rule that beat switching in the baseline audit, runs beside it on the same person.

        Read this first. The lab persons are invented: each copies the structure of one profile built from a public research dataset of support conversations (ESConv, CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial use), and every statement is written for the lab. They are not real people and not anyone's words. The simulated person failed its validation (Person Lab step 5), and matching the person's sensory words or echoing them showed no effect on people's own ratings (RAPPORT_ESCONV). This is a working demonstration of the architecture, built to be extended and tested, not evidence that it helps anyone.
