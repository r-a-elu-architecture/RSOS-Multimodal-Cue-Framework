# ATLAS on simulated people

Generated by `python -m rsso.atlas --evaluate`. 100 simulated people per row. Each has 12 possible triggers across the senses, 4 of them real (each adding 1.8 to 4.2 points of stress), and two that only trigger together (3 points). Ten moments a day are sampled, one in five a control moment; ratings are whole points with noise. Built and tested in simulation; effect unproven in people.

## The map (claims C93, C96)

| Days sampled | Real triggers found | Null nodes called triggers | The pair found to trigger only together | Map predicts unseen moments | Nodes with a stable weight (of 12) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 26.5% | 0.6% | 15% | 50% | 0 |
| 7 | 83.5% | 2.6% | 68% | 93% | 0.1 |
| 14 | 98% | 3% | 86% | 100% | 5.5 |

With no real triggers and no pair (7 days), 1.9% of nodes were called triggers, the pair was called a combination in 2%, and the map was said to predict in 0% of people.

## Transfer to untreated triggers (claims C94, C95)

Map, map again with no treatment, treat the strongest trigger (it falls by 80%), map a third time. Where the change spreads, untreated triggers fall by 40%; otherwise they do not. Every weight also falls by 15% from one mapping to the next by itself (habituation); the repeat mapping measures that drift and removes it.

| Truth | Repeat mapping before treatment | Days per mapping | Called spreads | Called stays | Unclear | Treated node did not change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spreads | yes | 7 | 23% | 0% | 51% | 20% |
| Spreads | yes | 14 | 67% | 0% | 28% | 5% |
| Spreads | no | 7 | 52% | 0% | 43% | 0% |
| Stays | yes | 7 | 9% | 0% | 65% | 20% |
| Stays | yes | 14 | 7% | 0% | 88% | 5% |
| Stays | no | 7 | 14% | 0% | 81% | 0% |

## The level sweep (claims C99, C100)

Sessions at each of five higher nodes by abstraction. Peaked: the benefit is highest at the middle level (identity, 2 points) and falls by 0.6 points a level either side; flat: 1 point at every level. Session-to-session noise 0.8 points.

| Truth | Sessions per level | Called peaks | Rises | Falls | Flat | Middle level found best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peaked | 4 | 24% | 1% | 1% | 74% | 64% |
| Peaked | 8 | 66% | 0% | 0% | 34% | 89% |
| Flat | 4 | 1% | 2% | 1% | 96% | 17% |
| Flat | 8 | 2% | 1% | 3% | 94% | 22% |

Reading: a week of sampling (ten moments a day) finds most real triggers and few false ones; combinations and stable weights need two weeks. Transfer needs two weeks per mapping to be seen in two thirds of people. Without a repeat mapping before treatment, habituation looks like transfer: a change that did not spread is called "spreads" twice as often. Showing that a change stayed put (less than half a point on untreated triggers) needs more data than three two-week mappings give; with this design the honest reading of a negative result is "unclear". The level sweep needs about eight sessions per level to tell a peak from a flat line.
