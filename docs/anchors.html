Anchors you can measure

An anchor is a cue paired with a state: a vibration code, a light, a scent, a phrase, a glyph. Here it becomes a measured object. Pair it at a peak, then test it with blind probes: the cue alone, or a sham cue, with your heart measured before and after. Its strength shows with an interval, and the page warns you when an anchor starts to take on stress instead of relieving it.

Heart rate from your camera

Rest the phone on a table, then cover the rear camera and the flash with a fingertip. Touch lightly (pressing hard squeezes the blood out), keep your hand still and warm, and breathe normally. On Android the flash turns on by itself; on iPhone, hold the finger against a bright light. About thirty seconds gives heart rate and, when the signal is very clean, heart rate variability (RMSSD).

Your measurements are used for captures and probes, and never leave this browser.

Chains

Anchors play in role order: healing, stability, redirection, lock-in. After each phase you rate how calm you are, and the report shows which phase did its job.

See it on simulated people

Four simulated anchors with known answers: a strong one, a weak one, one with no effect, and one taken over by use during stress. Each was paired eight times and probed eight times against four sham probes. Compare what the page concludes with the truth.

How well this works across many simulated people: rsso/results/peak.md. To run it with real people and instruments: the protocol kit.

PEAK is rsso/rsso/peak.py, identically docs/js/peak.js; camera heart rate is rsso/rsso/ppg.py, identically docs/js/ppg.js (tested). Specification: spec/PEAK.md. A strong anchor is a measured effect, not a treatment; if something feels wrong, stop and use the Safety page.
