# The autonomy gate on simulated people

Generated by `python -m rsso.gate --evaluate`. Simulated people over 60 days, each with one anchor of a known kind: strong, weak (below the smallest effect that counts), no effect, or strong until an outside stressor from day 25 pulls it negative (contaminated). The anchor is paired at a peak daily for 10 days and weekly after, probed every other day (one sham in three), and the device detects stress three times a day with 85% precision. With the gate, the device suggests or fires only at the stage the gate allows; without it, it fires automatically from day one. Built and tested in simulation; effect unproven in people.

| Anchor | Reached automatic (gate) | Day reached | Unhelpful automatic firings, with gate | Without gate | Helpful automatic firings, with gate | Without gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong | 100% | 11.4 | 19.5 | 27.8 | 105.7 | 152.2 |
| Weak | 15% | 19.7 | 1.4 | 180 | 0 | 0 |
| No effect | 0% | — | 0 | 180 | 0 | 0 |
| Contaminated from day 25 | 100% | 10.8 | 6.3 | 113.9 | 30.6 | 66.1 |

**Closing again.** After the outside stressor began on day 25, contaminated anchors lost automatic use in 75% of simulated people, within a day (the rest were already back at suggest when it began); 0% were automatic at day 60.

Reading: the gate trades a slower start (automatic firing begins after training, proof and confirmed offers) for far fewer firings that cannot help: anchors with no effect never fire on their own, weak ones rarely and briefly, and a contaminated anchor loses automatic use within days. Unhelpful firings that remain come from the detector's false alarms; a better detector lowers them further.

**Detector quality matters.** Strong anchors were still automatic at day 60 in 72.5% of simulated people with an 85%-precise detector, and in 20% with a 70%-precise one: the gate keeps a noisy detector at suggest, where the person confirms each offer.
