Run a session with RSOS

A structured session with your client's own nodes: what to say, what to do and what to measure at each step, the stop rules, the practice plan for between sessions, and a session record you can keep or add to research. RSOS supports your clinical judgement; it does not diagnose or treat.

      The decision matrix and the copilot

      A central meaning with three trigger cues, each with its own words, response and code. You learn the matrix by heart; the copilot reads the transcript line by line and lights up the branch it hears. After each line you mark what you actually chose, and the page measures how often you and the copilot agree, and how fast you answer.

      Built and tested in simulation. Effect unproven in people.

      Levels and the five-step chain

      Which level does a sentence speak from: environment, behaviour, skills, beliefs and values, identity or mission? The chain detects it, works one level above, offers the question for that level, passes it through the safety checks, and future-paces it. It is a word-list method: it helps you listen, it does not understand.

      Core Transformation, one question at a time

      Start from the unwanted state. Each answer leads to the question behind it, until a core state appears (peace, wholeness, being, love…) or seven steps are done. The chain becomes a program in which the core state flows back through every step.

        Re-living an event with the symbol and light

        After a difficult event: see it briefly and from a distance, let the symbol's light fill the scene, and rehearse what you would do now. Rate distress before and after. Imagery rescripting is an established method; doing it with the person's own symbol is what RSOS adds and tests.

        Overrides between sessions

        Each time fear, craving or an intrusive thought starts, log whether it was turned around and how many seconds the override took to start. OXQ is overrides per waking hour, times 100. An unusual perceptual experience is never a trigger for a cue: it goes to the clinician.

        Craving and context triggers

        A craving can start from a context: an app opening, arriving at a place, a time of day. When a context matches, the craving protocol is offered; the person starts it. Rate the craving at each exposure, and the page shows whether cue-reactivity falls over the months.

        Typology from speech

        Typologies are plug-ins that count kinds of words over a conversation: sensory words (seeing, hearing, feeling) or time orientation (past, present, future). It takes hundreds of words before a lean shows. Profiling another person's speech needs their consent, recorded first.

        The life timeline

        Scattered memories placed in order, each with a glyph. Mark whether you recall each as one specific moment (a day, a place) or only in general: memory specificity training aims to raise that share.

        Contexts, the four-day block and the setting

        A principle is learned in a four-day block: learn, rehearse, apply in real contexts, test without cues. Contexts can be recognised from speech ("the meeting starts") to fire a timed burst of the code. And the setting of each session (warmth, music, imagery) is logged, so that trials can show whether it matters.
