Identity, level by level

A symbol heads a tree of seven levels: mission, identity, values, beliefs, skills, behaviour and environment. Each level has its own statement, image and vibration code, so the whole profile can be played as one chain and called up by its symbol alone. Rate how true each level feels, and the page measures whether the profile is becoming yours, whether other people pull you away from it, and whether the behaviour starts in places you never trained.

Built and tested in simulationEffect unproven in people

The tree

Choose a level to write it. Tap the symbol or a code to feel it. Stored only in this browser.

How true is it today

Rate each level from 0 (not me at all) to 10 (completely me). Coherence is how true the levels feel together: their average, less how unevenly they are felt. Rate on ordinary days, and sometimes right after time with other people, to see whether you keep the profile while mirroring them.

Practice

The symbol by day, a few seconds, with the whole profile behind it. The whole chain at night, symbol down to environment, one level per breath. Four steps when an old state starts. Each opens in the player.

The prepared week

One level a day for seven days, each with a future situation. On the eighth day the symbol is installed in a light trance, with three future situations.

A month's dose

Whether practising in the identity's own language helps more than your first language is a trial you can run: An identity in another language.

The anchor tree

One central image with up to five contexts fixed to its parts, each with its own code: the mane is the meeting, the paws are the phone call. Walk the tree and recall each context from its part, timed. To compare, walk the same contexts from a single image.

Rehearsing a value conflict

Imagine, for a minute, a situation that goes against one of your values, with your heart measured before and during. Then play the profile's symbol and measure again. Rehearse an ordinary situation the same way, for comparison. A drop of 10% or more in heart rate variability counts as the body reacting.

Choose a mild situation, not a painful memory. You can skip this exercise, and stop at any moment if it feels too strong. If something feels wrong afterwards, use the Safety page.

Does it travel

A behaviour is learned when it starts in places it was never practised. Log each moment you meant to act from the profile: where, whether you had trained there, and whether the behaviour started.

Switching between profiles

Each profile is like a user on one operating system. Its login code calls it up; a schedule can switch profiles at set hours; the interrupt brings you back to your base profile at once, whatever is running. When a switch has settled, say so, and the page learns how long your switches take.

See it on a simulated person

A simulated person practised the steady profile for six months: every level became truer by a known amount each month, and time with other people pulled them away from it. Add the person to see what the page concludes, then compare with the truth.
