# PEAK: recovering known anchor strengths (simulation)

Generated by `python -m rsso.peak --evaluate`. Simulated people with known anchors (strong, weak, none, contaminated by use during stress), measured with probe trials: the cue alone against sham cues, RMSSD before and after, with realistic noise. Built and tested in simulation; effect unproven in people.

| Probes (cue + sham) | Strong found | No-effect anchors called an effect | Contamination flagged (sensitivity) | Healthy anchors flagged (1 - specificity) | Mean error of strength, % RMSSD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 + 4 | 84.0% | 2.0% | 90.0% | 0.7% | 2.3 |
| 8 + 4 | 100.0% | 0.0% | 100.0% | 0.7% | 2.2 |
| 12 + 4 | 98.0% | 0.0% | 100.0% | 0.7% | 1.8 |

Status given, by true kind (8 cue and 4 sham probes, 50 simulated anchors each). The contamination flag, not the status, is what catches contaminated anchors: their recent probes turn negative while older ones still pull the average up:

| True kind | strong | present | unclear | weakening | contaminated | building |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| strong | 50 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| weak | 3 | 1 | 46 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| none | 0 | 0 | 49 | 0 | 1 | 0 |
| contaminated | 0 | 0 | 25 | 25 | 0 | 0 |

Reading: with eight cue probes and four sham probes, a strong anchor is recognised, an anchor with no effect is rarely mistaken for one, and an anchor pulled negative by use during stress is flagged. Real people add sources of variance the simulation lacks (posture, breathing, time of day); the protocol kit (spec/kits/PEAK_PROTOCOL_KIT.md) measures them.

## Heart rate from the phone camera (simulation)

Synthetic fingertip signals at 30 frames a second with known beats, analysed by `rsso/ppg.py` (identical to `docs/js/ppg.js`). Heart rate is read reliably; RMSSD is approximate at 30 frames a second, so it is used only when the reading is very clean ("RMSSD usable"), and otherwise probes fall back to the heart-rate fall.
