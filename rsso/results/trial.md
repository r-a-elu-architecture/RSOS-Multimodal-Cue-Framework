# TRIAL: recovering known effects in personal randomised trials (simulation)

Generated by `python -m rsso.trial --evaluate`. Simulated people compare a real anchor with a sham code in permuted blocks; each session's calm change (0-10) carries noise (sd 1.5 points), a sleep effect and, where stated, drift over time. Built and tested in simulation; effect unproven in people.

| Blocks (sessions per arm) | A true difference of 1.5 points found | A difference claimed when there is none |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 17% | 2.5% |
| 8 | 42% | 3% |
| 12 | 63.5% | 4.5% |

**Pooling across 30 people** whose true effects vary around 1.0 point (sd 0.5), 8 blocks each: pooled estimate 1.14 [0.74, 1.54], between-person spread 0.81 (true 0.5).

**Drift and background factors.** 100 simulated people who improve by 0.15 points every session whatever the arm, with sleep moving calm by 0.6 points an hour; true difference 1.0. Average error of the raw difference 0.66; adjusted for session order and background factors 0.62. Random order keeps the raw difference fair; adjustment makes it more precise.

Reading: one person's trial rarely claims an effect that is not there (under 5%). Finding a real effect of 1.5 points needs about twelve sessions of each arm to succeed more often than not, so personal trials answer large effects for one person, and pooling many people answers moderate ones, with how much people differ. The protocol kit (spec/kits/TRIAL_PROTOCOL_KIT.md) runs the same design with real people.
