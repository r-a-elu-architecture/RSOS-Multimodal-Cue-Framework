# SLEEP and REWARD on simulated people

Generated by `python -m rsso.night --evaluate`. 60 simulated people per row. Built and tested in simulation; effect unproven in people.

## Cueing during sleep (claims C8, C36)

An 8-hour night of 30-second epochs with sleep cycles. The device reads the stage correctly 80% of the time in deep sleep (N3) and 40% in N1; a cue can wake the sleeper, more often in light sleep and at higher intensity. Closed loop: cue only after 90 seconds of N3 as read by the device, start low, lower the intensity after an arousal and pause five minutes, at most 300 cues. Random: a cue in a quarter of all epochs at the highest allowed intensity. Differences are against the same person's night with no cues.

| Policy | Cues | In deep sleep | Arousals caused | Extra time awake (min) | Deep sleep lost (min) | Cues that reached N2 or N3 without waking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Closed loop | 73.6 | 99.1% | 0.8 | 0.2 | 0.6 | 72.9 |
| Closed loop, poorer device (N3 read right 60%) | 33.4 | 92.2% | 0.3 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 33.2 |
| Random | 241.5 | 16% | 14.8 | 3.6 | 0.6 | 129.1 |

## Next-day recall, cued against uncued items (claims C7, C8)

Twenty items cued and twenty not, recalled in the evening and the next morning, over seven nights per person. Where cueing helps, cued items lose up to 6 points less retention overnight, in proportion to the cues that reached N2 or N3 without waking, and each cue-caused arousal costs a sixth of that.

| Truth | Policy | One person: called helps | One person: called hurts | Mean advantage (points) | Group of 20: called helps | Group of 20: called hurts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Helps | Closed loop | 15% | 0% | 4.6 | 100% | 0% |
| No effect | Closed loop | 6.7% | 0% | 0.5 | 5% | 0% |
| Helps | Random | 0% | 35% | -9.3 | 0% | 100% |

## Nightmares (claims C5, C33)

One nightmare a night on average, in REM, detected from a heart-rate surge in 80% of its epochs. The trauma-related code itself is never played.

| Response | Nightmare minutes | Without response | Wakings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety code | 0.6 | 2.8 | 0 |
| Gentle waking | 0.1 | 2.8 | 1 |

## Reward codes in training (claims C9, C28)

A 2 x 2 study over four weeks: reward codes on success or not, night replay or not; accuracy over 50 test shots. Where they work, reward codes add 4 points and night replay 2 points.

| Truth | Athletes | Reward effect found | Night-replay effect found |
|---|---|---|---|
| Both work | 40 | 40% | 13.3% |
| Both work | 80 | 63.3% | 25% |
| Neither works | 40 | 3.3% | 5% |
| Neither works | 80 | 1.7% | 3.3% |

A good move recognised from a wrist accelerometer by its shape: 90.3% of good moves, 0% of poor ones; the decision is made 150 ms after the release, so the reward code starts about 190 ms after it, within the 300 ms aim.

Reading: closed-loop cueing puts nearly every cue in deep sleep and rarely wakes the sleeper; random cueing at the allowed strength wakes them many times a night, adds time awake, and in the memory model turns a benefit into a loss (claim C36). A memory benefit of this size is found in 15% of single people over a week of nights, and in 100% of groups of 20: it is a group question. Reward codes and night replay are small effects: with 80 athletes the reward effect is found in 63.3% of studies and the replay effect in 25%.
