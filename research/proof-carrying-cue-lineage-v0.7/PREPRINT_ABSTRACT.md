# Proposed methods paper (abstract — draft, not submitted)

**Title:** Proof-Carrying Cue-Lineage Routing: Reconstructing Symbolic History and Enforcing Consent in a Human–AI Adaptive-System Prototype

**Abstract.** Adaptive human–AI systems that deliver individualized resource-state suggestions may rely on conversational context as a proxy for provenance. We evaluate a consent-gated, source-lineage-aware routing prototype derived from historical RSOS, RSIA, RSX, and RSSO design documents. A selected archive of 9,000 timestamped messages from 246 conversations was analyzed to distinguish chronological recency from available parent-linked ancestry. Among 3,052 usable parent links, the six-step symbolic feature history inferred chronologically disagreed with the available direct-ancestor history in 1,613 instances (52.85%), subject to substantial missing-parent and selection limitations. Indexed ancestry query results matched direct graph traversal in 60,000 comparisons, with a single-run speedup of 7.0×. In 90,000 synthetic attack cases over the recorded graph, the proof-carrying implementation made zero invalid targeted selections while accepting 7,493 contract-eligible cases; simpler recency and consent/sensor-only baselines made 2,434 and 12,171 invalid targeted selections, respectively. A separately implemented generic exact-provenance comparator performed identically to the RSOS-derived approach. A further 45,000-case holdout reproduced the qualitative results. Session-scoped, single-use authorization and audit tests rejected 120,000 adversarial request variants and detected 1,200 deliberately altered log records. The findings validate a **software conformance and provenance architecture**, not neurological mechanisms or treatment effectiveness. We release a public synthetic fixture, standard-library source code, aggregate results, and an adversarial test harness for independent adaptation and falsification.

**Keywords:** provenance; event sourcing; human–AI co-adaptation; safety shield; resource-state proposals; consent; adversarial evaluation; BCI methods.
